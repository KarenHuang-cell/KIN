The brief

Innovation under pressure.

A timeline nobody had attempted.

When Singapore was selected to host the prestigious World Aquatics Championships in 2025, the engineering challenge was unprecedented: transform multiple venues across the island into world-class aquatic facilities capable of hosting 2,500 elite athletes from nearly 200 countries — all in under six months.

KIN was appointed Official Venue Overlay Planning Service Partner and Main Contractor for design & build. From a 250,000 sq ft car park converted to championship arena, to Southeast Asia’s first 37‑metre high diving tower, to a fully‑operational International Broadcast Centre stood up in 1.5 weeks — the brief was precision delivery on impossible timelines.

Singapore won the right to host the 2025 World Aquatics Championships with one of the most ambitious overlay programmes ever staged in the region: 2,500 athletes, three venues, a six‑month build window. Chapter 01

Car park to championship arena.

The centrepiece was a 250,000 sq ft car park next to Leisure Park Kallang, converted into the WCH Arena in six months. The competition pool — 50m long, 26m wide, 3m deep, holding 3.9 million litres — was engineered to reduce turbulence and optimise conditions for world-record performances.

Around 4,800 spectators were accommodated with optimised sightlines and 177 metres of LED screens, including a 29‑metre screen on the south wall.

WCH Arena, Singapore Sports Hub. 177m of LED · 4,800 seats · 3.9M litres.
Build

A pool that holds 3.9 million litres, engineered to reduce turbulence under the eye of world records.

Chapter 02

A 37‑metre first for Southeast Asia.

At Palawan Green, Sentosa, KIN constructed Southeast Asia’s first high diving tower — a 37‑metre temporary structure built on a 21‑tonne steel foundation comprising 108 precision‑manufactured curved panels secured by 6,400 bolts. Panel expansion was monitored to the millimetre as the pool filled.

Singapore’s tropical climate added pressure: UV‑tinted film, additional cooling, and specialised chemical treatments were deployed to keep the venue competition‑ready. High diving sessions sold out entirely.

High diving tower, Palawan Green, Sentosa. 21t steel base · 108 panels · 6,400 bolts.
Broadcast

11 languages. 20 countries. One broadcast hub, stood up in 1.5 weeks.

Chapter 03

Broadcasting to 150 territories.

Perhaps the most complex challenge was building a fully‑functional International Broadcast Centre inside OCBC Arena Hall in just 1.5 weeks — handling live coverage from all venues and broadcasting to 150 territories worldwide.

KIN installed precision air conditioning for sensitive equipment, engineered STC40‑rated soundproof walls, and implemented double‑layer tempered glass panels to maintain visual connectivity without compromising audio quality. Dual synchronised generators ensured zero downtime, while custom cable‑tray systems and high‑speed fibre linked all venues to the central hub.

International Broadcast Centre, OCBC Arena Hall. STC40 walls · dual generators · 1.5 weeks to live.
Logistics & care

Working around what we couldn’t move.

2 AM gangway operations

Installing gangways for the open‑water swimming venue required a coordinated 2 AM operation with police and ranger escorts — navigating Sentosa’s spatial and environmental constraints when the island was at its quietest.

Designing around resident trees

KIN worked closely with Sentosa Development Corporation to protect resident trees with complex root systems — designing the venue layout around natural features rather than removing them.
