FISE Singapore 2025 — Chart Studies

Four ways to draw the same pie.

Same data, same palette, four distinct editorial stances. Each variation answers the question "how much weight should the chart carry on the page?" differently — from quiet caption to full-spread statement.

01 — EDITORIAL CALLOUTS

Solid pie, leader-line callouts

A classic editorial pie with hairline leader lines that fan out to perimeter labels. The most magazine-grade option — reads like a New York Times business graphic or an FT chart-of-the-day. Chart and legend are one object; no separate ledger needed.

02 — IN-SLICE TYPOGRAPHY

Bold, the numbers live in the slices

Percentages sit directly inside their slices, with paper-colored hairline gaps separating them. The loudest, most poster-like option — turns the chart into a visual statement. Works because three of the four slices are large enough to hold typography; smaller slices (Print, Broadcast) get scaled-down labels to stay legible.

03 — PIE + RANKED BARS

Pie for the shape, bars for the ranking

A solid pie carries the "share of whole" read; sorted horizontal bars on the right let the eye compare exact magnitudes (which pie slices, especially small ones, are genuinely bad at). The most analytical option — feels like a Bloomberg report or McKinsey deck. Each bar can carry sub-text without crowding the chart.

04 — HALF-PIE FAN

A 180° fan, like a press dashboard meter

A solid semicircle that arcs across the page — labels and percentages sit cleanly below the chart, mirroring the stat ledger at the top of the case study. The most editorial-magazine option, and the one that integrates best with the rest of the page's typographic system. Reads as a measurement, not a pie.
